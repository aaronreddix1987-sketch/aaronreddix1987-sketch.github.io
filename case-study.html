Where we started

Busy, but leaking

Like a lot of small businesses, we had built fast. There were dozens of web pages, thousands of files and a long list of products. It looked like progress.

But nobody had checked the whole thing from the customer's side. A buyer could land on an old page, press a button and not know what would happen next. That is the same leak as a phone that rings with nobody there to answer.

What we checked

  • Company files. Sorted into what is current and what is old.
  • Checkout links. Opened and compared with the page that pointed to them.
  • Delivery. What should the buyer receive after paying?
  • Pages. Is it true, current and something we can stand behind?

What we did

Audit. Transfer. Fill.

These are the three steps of the Buyback Loop from Dan Martell's book Buy Back Your Time. We used them on ourselves.

Audit

We followed the path a customer takes, link by link. Three buttons opened the wrong product. Most titles in the store were not finished enough to deliver well.

Transfer

Checking the site moved from memory to a machine. An automatic check now tests selected pages and checkout links every six hours and raises a flag if anything breaks.

Fill

The time goes into one offer we can deliver well: the 7-day missed-call pilot. Everything else waits until it is ready.
