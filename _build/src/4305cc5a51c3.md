# How to Use This Guide

This short guide comes with the **Lead Leak Calculator & Missed-Call Tracker**, a Google Sheets file. The link to make your own copy is on the download page you reached after checkout. The spreadsheet does the arithmetic; this guide explains how to fill it in honestly and what to do with the answer.

It is written for owner-run local service businesses: plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofers, cleaners, landscapers, movers, auto shops, salons, and anyone else whose new customers usually start with a phone call.

:::warning Estimates, not promises
The calculator multiplies the numbers you enter. If the inputs are guesses, the answer is a guess. The example numbers in the sheet are made up. Total Transformation Inc. (TTI) does not promise any revenue, savings or result. The purpose is to replace guesses with your own counted numbers within two weeks.
:::

# Chapter 1: Why Missed Calls Are Hard to See

:::objectives
- Explain why missed-call losses rarely show up in your books.
- Separate missed calls into useful categories.
- Set up the spreadsheet in ten minutes.
:::

## The invisible line item

A missed call that becomes a lost job never appears on an invoice, a bank statement or a tax return. You only see the jobs you won. That is why owners often underestimate the cost of calls that go unanswered while they are on a ladder, under a sink or driving between jobs. The calculator puts a rough number on something you cannot otherwise see.

## Not every missed call is a lost customer

Many missed calls are spam, vendors, or existing customers who will call back. The calculator only counts **new potential customers** who **never reach you later**. Keeping those categories separate is what makes the estimate honest.

| Category | Counts as a possible lost job? |
|---|---|
| New potential customer who never reaches you | Yes |
| New potential customer you call back and reach | No, if you reach them (log the outcome) |
| Existing customer | Usually no; log it anyway |
| Spam or sales call | No |
| Vendor | No |

## Ten-minute set-up

1. Open the link on your download page and choose **File > Make a copy**.
2. Open the **Lead Leak Calculator** tab. Replace the blue example numbers with your best guesses.
3. Open **Weekly Summary** and type the Monday date of this week in cell A2.
4. Put a reminder on your phone at the end of each workday: "Log missed calls."

# Chapter 2: Filling In the Calculator

:::objectives
- Understand each input and where to find it.
- Read each result line.
- Use the stress tests to see how fragile the estimate is.
:::

## The inputs

| Input | How to estimate it |
|---|---|
| Missed or unanswered calls per week | Count them in your phone's recent-calls list for one normal week |
| Share that are new potential customers | Of those calls, how many were people who had not hired you before |
| Share who never reach you later | Your honest guess on day 1; replace it with your log on day 15 |
| Share you usually win when you reach a new caller | Quotes that became jobs, from your own history |
| Average job value | Average first invoice for a new customer |
| Gross margin | What is left after materials and direct labour |
| Monthly cost of a fix | Any answering service, staff time or software you are considering |

## The results

The sheet works through the arithmetic one line at a time:

1. **Missed new-customer calls per month** = calls per week × share new × 52 ÷ 12.
2. **Callers who never reach you** = line 1 × share who never reach you.
3. **Jobs they might have become** = line 2 × your win rate.
4. **Revenue at risk per month** = line 3 × average job value.
5. **Gross profit at risk per month** = line 4 × gross margin.
6. **Extra jobs per month needed to cover the fix** = monthly fix cost ÷ (job value × margin).

:::example Illustrative: the example numbers in the sheet
Twelve missed calls a week, half from new potential customers, 60% of whom never reach the owner later, a 30% win rate, a $450 average job and a 40% margin. That gives about 26 missed new-customer calls a month, about 15.6 who never reach the owner, about 4.7 jobs, about $2,106 of revenue at risk and about $842 of gross profit at risk per month. Covering a $297-a-month fix would need about 1.7 extra jobs a month at those numbers. These are made-up inputs, not typical results.
:::

## Stress tests

Two stress-test lines show how the answer changes if only half as many callers are really lost, or if your win rate is 10 points lower. In the example, gross profit at risk falls to about $421 and about $562. If a decision only makes sense at your most optimistic guess, wait for the log.

# Chapter 3: Two Weeks of Logging

:::objectives
- Log missed calls quickly and consistently.
- Call back fast with simple, honest scripts.
- Read the Weekly Summary.
:::

## What to log

Each missed or unanswered call gets one row in the **Missed-Call Log**: date, time, caller type, what they needed (a few words), whether you called back, minutes until you called back, outcome and job value if booked. Do not write customers' full names or numbers in a shared file; your phone already has them.

## Calling back

Speed and courtesy matter more than a perfect script. The **Callback Scripts** tab has short scripts for answered callbacks, voicemail, a text after a missed call, after-hours follow-up, quote follow-up and a single review request after a finished job.

:::warning Texting rules
Only text a number that called you or gave you permission to text, include opt-out wording, and stop at once when asked. Business calls and texts are covered by the Telephone Consumer Protection Act and state laws [1]. Ask a professional if you are unsure.
:::

## Reading the Weekly Summary

The summary counts each week automatically from your log: missed calls, new callers, callbacks, callback rate, average minutes to callback, booked jobs, booked value and lost or no-answer calls. After two weeks you can replace the calculator's guesses:

| Calculator input | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Missed calls per week | Average of the two weeks' Missed calls |
| Share new | New callers ÷ Missed calls |
| Share who never reach you | Lost or no-answer among new callers ÷ New callers |
| Win rate | Booked ÷ new callers you reached |

:::checklist Daily logging habit
- Every missed call has a row before I finish work.
- Spam is marked Spam, not Lost.
- Every new caller got a callback or a text the same day.
- Outcome is filled in once known.
:::

:::summary
Two weeks of two-minute logging turns guesses into counts. Call back quickly, use honest scripts, follow texting rules, and let the Weekly Summary do the counting.
:::

# Chapter 4: Deciding What to Do

:::objectives
- Choose between doing nothing, changing habits and adding coverage.
- Compare options on cost per month against gross profit at risk.
- Set a review date.
:::

## Three honest options

| Option | When it fits | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | Logged leak is small or calls are mostly spam | None |
| Change habits | Most lost callers came at predictable times (lunch, drive time) | Your time: block callback windows, set voicemail expectations |
| Add coverage | Leak is larger than the cost of coverage after the stress tests | Answering service, part-time help, or AI answering software |

## A simple decision rule

If **logged** gross profit at risk per month is clearly larger than the monthly cost of a fix, even under the stress tests, coverage is worth testing. If it is smaller, change habits first and log again in a month.

:::example Illustrative: two shops, two answers
Shop A logs 14 missed calls a week, 8 from new callers, and loses 5 of them; its numbers show about $1,000 of gross profit at risk a month, so it tests coverage for a month. Shop B logs 9 missed calls, mostly spam and existing customers, and loses one new caller in two weeks; it blocks two callback windows a day instead and spends nothing. Both decisions come from their own logs.
:::
