# How to Use This Prompt Desk

This is a working set of 150 prompts for licensed real estate agents, team leads and transaction coordinators who use AI chat tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. It is organised around the jobs agents already use AI for. According to the National Association of REALTORS® 2026 technology survey, as summarised by HousingWire, 23% of agents use AI daily and 25% weekly; among users, 75% use it for listing descriptions, 56% for social media and 52% for emails, and 63% named the learning curve as their biggest issue [1]. A separate survey reported by NAR found accuracy and compliance were top concerns for agents using AI [2]. This desk is built around those two worries: get drafts quickly, and check them before they go out.

:::warning Read this first
This prompt desk is educational. It is not legal advice. Fair housing law, advertising rules, MLS rules and brokerage policies vary and change. Review every AI draft before you publish, follow your brokerage's AI policy, and ask your broker or an attorney when unsure. Total Transformation Inc. (TTI) makes no promise of leads, listings, sales or income.
:::

## The three rules behind every prompt

1. **Give facts, do not ask for them.** AI tools do not know your listing, your market or your client. Paste the facts. Prompts use [BRACKETS] for what you fill in.
2. **Describe the property, never the people.** The Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to publish an advertisement for the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates any preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin [3]. Many states and cities protect more classes. The prompts tell the AI to avoid these preferences, but you are responsible for the result.
3. **Review before you publish.** NAR's Code of Ethics Article 12 requires REALTORS® to be honest and truthful in their real estate communications and to present a true picture in their advertising [4]. Run the Fair Housing and Accuracy Review prompts (Section 3) on anything public.

## Privacy

Do not paste client Social Security numbers, bank details, full contracts with personal data, or anything your brokerage forbids into a public AI tool. Use first names or initials and remove details that are not needed for the draft. Check whether your AI tool's settings allow it to train on your inputs.

# Section 1: Set-Up Prompts

:::objectives
- Write a reusable Agent Brief that gives every chat the context it needs.
- Set a house style so drafts sound like you.
- Install the fair housing and accuracy guardrails at the start of a chat.
:::

## P001 Agent Brief (paste at the start of every chat)

"You are my drafting assistant. I am [NAME], a licensed real estate agent with [BROKERAGE], licensed in [STATE]. I mostly serve [BUYERS/SELLERS/BOTH] in [MARKET/AREA] at [PRICE RANGE/PROPERTY TYPES]. Rules for everything you write for me: (1) Describe properties and places, never the kind of person who should live there. Do not mention or imply race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin, or any other protected class, and avoid phrases like 'perfect for families', 'walking distance to church', 'exclusive neighbourhood' or 'ideal for young professionals'. (2) Use only facts I give you. If something is missing, write [CHECK: what is missing] instead of guessing. (3) No superlatives I cannot prove ('best', 'safest', 'top-rated schools') and no promises about value or returns. Reply 'Ready' and wait for my first task."

## P002 House style

"Here are three things I have written that sound like me: [PASTE 3 SHORT SAMPLES]. Describe my writing style in 6 bullet points (sentence length, tone, words I use, words I avoid, formatting, sign-offs). Use that style in all drafts in this chat."

## P003 Words I never use

"Add these to your never-use list for this chat: [LIST, e.g. 'dream home', 'won't last', 'motivated seller' unless I say so]. Also avoid [STATE/BROKERAGE-SPECIFIC TERMS]. Confirm the full list back to me."

## P004 Brokerage disclosure line

"Draft three short versions (under 20 words, under 40 words, under 80 words) of a footer that shows I am a licensed agent with [BROKERAGE], [LICENSE NUMBER if required in my state], [OFFICE ADDRESS/PHONE if required]. Mark anything my state might require that I have not given you as [CHECK]."

## P005 Accuracy contract

"For the rest of this chat: after every draft, add a short list titled 'Facts to verify' that names every number, date, feature, distance and claim in the draft, so I can check each one before I publish."

# Section 3: Fair Housing and Accuracy Review Prompts

:::objectives
- Catch fair housing problems before anything is published.
- Find unverified claims and exaggerations.
- Keep a record that you reviewed AI drafts.
:::

HUD has warned that advertisers using AI and targeted-ad platforms can still violate the Fair Housing Act, including through ad delivery that excludes people based on protected characteristics [5][6]. Use these review prompts on every public draft, and then read the draft yourself.

## P026 Fair housing review

"Review this draft for fair housing risk under the federal Fair Housing Act and common state additions. Flag any word or phrase that describes or implies a preferred or excluded buyer or tenant, including familial status, disability, religion, national origin, sex, race or colour, and any steering language about neighbourhoods or schools. For each flag, suggest neutral wording that describes the property. Draft: [PASTE]."

## P027 Accuracy review

"List every factual claim in this draft (numbers, dates, features, distances, conditions, school or neighbourhood statements). For each, tell me whether it appears in this fact sheet: [PASTE FACT SHEET]. Draft: [PASTE DRAFT]."

## P028 Superlative and promise scan

"Find any superlatives, guarantees, investment-return claims or urgency phrases in this draft and rewrite them as neutral, verifiable statements: [PASTE]."

## P029 Tone check

"Rate this draft for clarity and professional tone on a 1 to 5 scale and suggest three edits that would make it plainer: [PASTE]."

## P030 Readability

"Rewrite this so a busy reader understands it in one read: sentences under 20 words, no jargon, same facts: [PASTE]."

## P031 Disclosure check

"Does this ad include the brokerage and licence information I listed in my Agent Brief? If anything required may be missing, list it as [CHECK]: [PASTE AD]."

## P032 Ad targeting reminder

"I plan to run this as a paid social ad: [PASTE]. Write a checklist of targeting and audience settings I should review so the ad is not limited by age, gender, ZIP code or other characteristics that could raise fair housing concerns, and remind me to check the platform's special ad category rules for housing."

## P033 Image description check

"Here is how I plan to describe the photos and video in this post: [DESCRIPTIONS]. Flag anything that features or implies a preferred type of resident and suggest property-focused alternatives."

## P034 Review log entry

"Create a one-line review log entry for this draft with date, channel, prompts used (P026, P027), issues found and fixes made: [NOTES]."

## P035 Final pre-publish checklist

"Turn my notes into a five-item pre-publish checklist for this piece: facts verified, fair housing reviewed, disclosures included, photos checked, broker approval if needed: [NOTES]."

# Appendix B: Fair Housing Review Card

:::checklist Before anything goes public
- Describes the property, not the people who should live there.
- No mention or implication of race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin, or other classes protected in my state and city.
- No steering descriptions of neighbourhoods, schools or residents.
- Every number, date, size and feature claim is verified.
- No unprovable superlatives or pressure language.
- Brokerage name and required disclosures included.
- Photos, captions and alt text reviewed.
- Paid ad audience settings reviewed against the platform's housing-ad policy.
- AI-generated or edited media labelled where required.
:::

:::callout Swaps that keep the meaning and lose the risk
| Avoid | Try instead |
|---|---|
| Perfect for families | Three bedrooms and a fenced yard |
| Walking distance to church | 0.3 miles to [named street or landmark] |
| Exclusive, private community | Gated entry; HOA-maintained grounds |
| Great for young professionals | 10-minute drive to downtown |
| Safe neighbourhood | Cul-de-sac location |
| Bachelor pad | One-bedroom unit |
| Mother-in-law suite | Detached accessory unit with kitchenette |
:::

# Appendix C: Prompt-Writing Formula

Most good prompts have five parts. Use them to write your own.

| Part | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Sets the job | "You are my listing copy assistant." |
| Facts | Gives the only material to use | "Use only this fact sheet: ..." |
| Task | Says exactly what to produce | "Write MLS remarks under 1,000 characters." |
| Rules | Sets limits | "Features only; no superlatives; mark gaps as [CHECK]." |
| Check | Forces a review step | "Then list facts to verify." |
