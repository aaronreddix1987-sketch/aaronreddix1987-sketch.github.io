Business Readiness

Know what to check before you open, take on a new service or apply for funding. Start with the step that fits your business.

Free education. No sign-up, credit check or application.

Jump to checklist

  1. Registration and permits
  2. Funding preparation
  3. Scams and credit reports

01 / Set up

Check registrations and permits

  • California: Use the Secretary of State’s startup guide to check which filings fit your business structure.
  • City of Los Angeles: If you conduct business in the city, check the Business Tax Registration Certificate (BTRC) requirements and use the Office of Finance for current filing instructions. If you operate elsewhere, check that jurisdiction instead.
  • Business name: If you use a different name for business, check whether a Fictitious Business Name filing (often called a DBA) is required. The LA County Clerk explains who should file.
  • Licenses and permits: Registration does not replace industry permits. Use CalGold to find the issuing agencies and confirm requirements directly. If you plan to offer towing or vehicle transport, separately verify the permits and insurance required for your exact services and location before taking jobs.

02 / Prepare

Get your funding documents in order

  • Keep separate records: Use separate business banking rather than mixing business and personal funds. If you run multiple legal entities, keep each entity’s accounts, books, contracts and filings separate.
  • Gather the basics: Have formation and ownership records, tax ID confirmation and any required licenses available. A lender may ask for bank statements, tax returns, financial statements or other documentation. Ask for its checklist first; send sensitive records only through a verified secure channel.
  • Write down the amount and why you need it: Make a short business plan, list expenses and estimate cash flow and repayment. Review SBA funding options and compare written terms, total cost, fees and any personal guarantee before signing. Being prepared does not guarantee approval.

03 / Verify

Spot scams and check credit errors

  • Pause before paying: Verify unexpected invoices and official-looking notices using a contact you found independently. Be wary of guaranteed funding, requests for money in exchange for approval and pressure to sign immediately. See the FTC guide for small businesses or report suspected fraud to the FTC.
  • Dispute actual errors: If something is wrong on your personal credit report, follow the CFPB’s credit report dispute guide. It explains how to contact the credit bureau and the company that provided the information.
